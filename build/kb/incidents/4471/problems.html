What each one is

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first, so a booking for 7 April comes out as 4 July. Every report that names both dates is an exact day-and-month swap. This is the fix #4471 has queued.

hint: the booking-v3 form asks for the month first. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what it tells them, and the form reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. The swap looks the same as the parser’s, but it comes only through that form, and only for the tenants that run it. A parser fix does not touch it; the hint has to follow the tenant’s locale.

shift: the date moves by one day. “Tuesday 12 May showed as Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday, client in Toronto got Monday.” “The invite arrived before I made the booking.” No day-and-month swap does that; a date that crosses a time zone, or a daylight-saving change, does. The desk already lists these reports in its notes, and #4471 still counted them under the parser.

How the split is made

  • One report per id: the export repeats some rows.
  • The tenant decides, not intake. intakeTag is a guess from the reporter’s browser and disagrees with the tenant on dozens of these reports, so this page never reads it.
  • Counted the desk’s way, as CauseTable does: every report under its cause (causes.ts), by tenant locale, booking form and channel. shift is the desk’s own list of one-day moves (desk-notes.json); those reports also stay counted where they’re filed.

Outcome

Engineering closed #4471 on 5 October.

  • parser: fixed, fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales. #1 closed.
  • hint: won’t fix. The booking-v3 form is retired. #2 closed.
  • shift: no fix shipped; the parser fix does not move dates across time zones. #3 closed; reopen it if one-day moves come back.

Lessons

  • Subjects can match while causes differ. A count is only as true as the reason that groups it.
  • Before marking a big ticket known, read a few reports from each tenant, form and channel.
